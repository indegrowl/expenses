# Case Study: YNAB category setups (Mandatory vs Optional, Themed, Paycheck-based)

## Who / source
YNAB (You Need A Budget) shares three beginner-friendly ways to structure categories.

## The categorization idea
Instead of obsessing over the *perfect* category tree, you pick a structure that matches your current problem:

### 1) Mandatory vs Optional
Two big buckets:
- **Mandatory**: rent, phone, groceries, gas, utilities
- **Optional**: vacation, eating out, movies, “daily cinnamon bun”

This is basically “survival + sanity”. It’s meant to reduce overwhelm and quickly show what’s negotiable.

### 2) Themed budget
Group by life area:
- Housing, Transportation, Kids, Pets, Household, Subscriptions, etc.

This is good when you’re stable and you want to optimize / understand where money goes.

### 3) Pin it to the paycheck
Organize by which paycheck covers the expense:
- Paycheck 1: electric, internet, car payment, student loan
- Paycheck 2: rent, phone bill, premiums
- Everyday: groceries, dining out
- Non-monthly: holidays, annual subscriptions, car registration

This is designed to break the paycheck-to-paycheck cycle.

## Practical takeaways
- Pick a category structure based on your *failure mode* (overwhelm vs cashflow timing vs visibility).
- “Non-monthly / true expenses” as a first-class concept prevents surprise spikes.

## How this maps to hledger
- Structure your expense accounts to match one of the above.
- You can keep the hledger account tree stable while changing how you *report* (e.g., grouping in reports).
